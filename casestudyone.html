The Stakes


Jupiter Money's home screen is the first thing every user sees, multiple times a day, for one core purpose: checking their money. But for new earners — users with their first salary, low financial literacy, and a tight open-check-pay-close loop — that core task was buried under promotional content and lower-priority features, and Play Store reviews confirmed it was costing trust, not just convenience.

The Problem Statement



New-earner Jupiter users fail to quickly see their balance and spending from the home screen because the layout prioritizes promotional content and Pots above the user's own financial information — resulting in balance requiring extra navigation, spend totals buried below the fold, and user-reported frustration across multiple reviews.

Options Considered

Option A — Reorder hierarchy: Rebalance the screen so balance/spending sits alongside Pots near the top, with promotional content demoted but not removed.

The Decision + Tradeoff


Balance replaces spend-total as the home screen's primary financial signal. In the original design, a spend-total figure ('Total spends this month') existed on the home screen but was buried as the 4th element, requiring a scroll to reach. Rather than simply relocating that same spend-total higher up, we replaced it with the account balance — positioned side-by-side with Pots — because balance is the more immediate, higher-frequency check for someone in an open-check-pay-close loop, while spend total remains one tap away via 'View Analysis.'

Tradeoff: users no longer see a spend summary on first glance without a tap; accepted because balance visibility was the more urgent unmet need, and spend total was already present in the original, whereas balance was entirely absent from the home screen before.

Outcome


This is a personal case study, not a shipped product, so these are measured design changes and reasoned estimates, not live user data.


Measured:

  1. Balance visibility: reduced from 1 tap (Home → Money section) to 0 taps (visible immediately on Home).

  2. Spend visibility: in the original, a spend-total figure existed but was buried below Pots and Insurance, requiring a scroll to reach. The redesign replaces this with account balance surfaced directly on the home screen (0 taps), with full spend analysis one tap away via 'View Analysis' — trading a buried summary number for immediate balance visibility plus quick access to detail.

  3. Pots quick-actions: reduced from 4 surfaced options to 1 primary action (Add Money), a 75% reduction in home-screen choices, with full functionality preserved one tap deeper via "View all Pots".


Estimated: For a user checking their balance multiple times a day — a reasonable pattern for someone closely tracking a first salary — removing the extra tap and scroll is estimated to save a few seconds per check, compounding to a few minutes saved per month. This is a projected estimate based on the step reduction, not measured session data.

What's Next


During research, I identified a separate issue — Pots intermittently disappearing from navigation across screens (reported in playstore review) — pointing to a navigation-consistency problem distinct from this home-screen hierarchy fix. I scoped this out to keep the case study focused, but it's the natural next problem to investigate for this product.
